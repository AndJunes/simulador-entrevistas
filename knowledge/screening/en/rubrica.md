## How to use this rubric

Score each criterion from 1 to 5. Structured interviewing guidance recommends three things [K09][K10]:
- a shared scale applied the same way to every answer;
- written examples of what each level looks like;
- evidence-based scoring.

OPM recommends one scale for all competencies, usually with 3 to 7 levels [K10]. Google uses four levels: poor, borderline, solid and outstanding [K09].

The 1–5 scale here maps to those levels:
- 1 = poor
- 2 = borderline
- 3 = solid but improvable
- 4 = solid
- 5 = outstanding

⚠ sin fuente — heurística: the exact anchor wording below is the corpus author's own synthesis of the cited guidance. It is not a published rubric.

**Non-applicable criteria** [K42]: `motivation_company_knowledge` applies only to motivation or company questions, and `professionalism_logistics` only to logistics questions (availability, contract, compensation). When they don't apply they are not scored or averaged. Logistics questions are **not** scored as a competency: they are reported separately as logistics alignment.

Always quote the part of the transcript that justifies the score. Red flags are reported as a separate checklist (last section), not as a scored criterion.

## clarity_structure

Does the answer have a recognizable structure, so a listener can follow it on a call?
- For the pitch, the structure is present–past–future [K04] or the 4-part version in [K06].
- For examples, STAR is the structure [K10].

| Score | Descriptor |
|---|---|
| 1 | No discernible structure. Jumps between topics, and the main point is unclear or missing. |
| 2 | Some structure, but the point appears late or is buried in detail. The listener must work to follow. |
| 3 | Clear opening point and a mostly logical order. One or two detours. |
| 4 | Clear structure (e.g. present → past → future, or S-T-A-R). Each part is easy to identify. Ends with a clear close. |
| 5 | Leads with the answer, follows a clean structure, and signposts transitions ("first…, then…, as a result…"). Close ties back to the role. |

## conciseness

Time is scarce in a 15-minute call. Career centers advise 60–90 s for "tell me about yourself" [K05][K06]. Rambling or unfocused answers are listed as a common mistake [K14]. For behavioral examples, see the STAR corpus.

| Score | Descriptor |
|---|---|
| 1 | Far outside the target length (e.g. a pitch over ~3 min, or a one-line answer to an open question). Repeats itself. |
| 2 | Noticeably long or short for the question type. Contains filler content that adds nothing. |
| 3 | Near the target length, with some unnecessary detail. |
| 4 | Within the target length. Every sentence supports the point. |
| 5 | Within the target length, with key information first. Invites follow-up instead of front-loading every detail. |

## motivation_company_knowledge

Recruiters probe what motivates a candidate and look past location or pay as the only reasons [K08]. Career guides tell candidates to research the organization's mission, products and goals before the interview [K07]. Asking "what do you do here?" is a listed mistake [K14].

| Score | Descriptor |
|---|---|
| 1 | No reason beyond "I need a job" or pay. No knowledge of what the company builds. |
| 2 | Generic motivation ("it's a great company", "I like tech") that could fit any employer. |
| 3 | Mentions one specific fact about the company or product, loosely tied to personal interest. |
| 4 | Specific knowledge of the product or domain, clearly linked to the candidate's skills and goals. |
| 5 | Specific, accurate knowledge, a credible personal link (e.g. past work with similar tech), and a forward-looking reason to join this team. |

## experience_role_alignment

Recruiters check that candidates can clearly describe past work and how they applied their skills. They also check that the candidate understands the role [K08]. The O*NET profile of software developers describes the core of the job [K11]:
- analyzing requirements;
- designing and testing software;
- modifying software to fix errors or improve performance;
- working with other engineers.

Career centers advise connecting your skills to the employer's needs instead of listing them separately [K14].

| Score | Descriptor |
|---|---|
| 1 | Experience described is unrelated to backend work, or so vague it can't be evaluated. |
| 2 | Lists technologies (Node.js, Rust, SQL) without saying what was built or what the candidate did. |
| 3 | Describes at least one relevant project with the candidate's role. The link to the job is implicit. |
| 4 | Describes relevant backend work (APIs, data, services, bugs) with own role, tools and a result. Explicitly links it to the job requirements. |
| 5 | As 4, plus honest scope ("I built X, the team owned Y") and a measurable or verifiable result. Identifies which job requirements it covers and which are still being learned. |

## communication_english

This criterion evaluates **fluency, intelligibility and coherence, NOT accent**. Munro & Derwing found that strongly accented L2 speech was often still highly intelligible and comprehensible [K20]. Penalizing accent is therefore not justified.

Perceived fluency is predicted mainly by pauses and speed, with repairs contributing very little [K21]. The anchors draw on the CEFR spoken-language descriptors [K19]:
- **B1:** keeps going comprehensibly, but pausing for planning and repair is very evident.
- **B2:** fairly even tempo, few noticeably long pauses, can link ideas with a limited set of connectors, with some "jumpiness".
- **C1:** fluent, almost effortless, well-structured speech.

SHRM also flags over-rehearsed delivery as a communication concern [K08].

| Score | Descriptor (combine with the deterministic form metrics: pauses, wpm, fillers) |
|---|---|
| 1 | Message often unclear. Frequent long pauses break ideas apart. The listener must guess meaning (below CEFR B1-like). |
| 2 | Understandable with effort. Very evident pausing for planning or repair. Short, disconnected sentences (B1-like). |
| 3 | Clear overall, with some hesitation while searching for words. Ideas linked with simple connectors (B2-like, with jumpiness). |
| 4 | Fairly even tempo with few long pauses. Clear, coherent discourse. Errors don't cause misunderstanding (solid B2). |
| 5 | Fluent and spontaneous, with smooth flow and varied connectors. Sounds conversational, not memorized (C1-like). |

## professionalism_logistics

This criterion covers:
- **Availability:** start date, time-zone overlap, contract type.
- **Compensation handling.**
- **Other processes.**
- **Questions for the recruiter.**

Recruiters ask about pay expectations and location [K08]. Career guidance on compensation [K16][K17]:
- research market ranges beforehand;
- consider total compensation;
- answer with a researched range, or ask for the role's budgeted band.

Asking questions shows interest, and closing with "what are the next steps?" is standard advice [K13]. Not having questions is a listed mistake [K14].

| Score | Descriptor |
|---|---|
| 1 | Can't state availability or expectations. Evasive or inconsistent. No questions for the recruiter. |
| 2 | Gives logistics only when pushed. Names a number with no basis, or refuses to engage. Questions are generic or answered on the website. |
| 3 | States availability and a figure or range. One relevant question. |
| 4 | Clear availability and time-zone overlap. Researched range stated calmly (hourly or monthly for a contract), with openness to discuss. Asks 2–3 relevant questions including next steps. |
| 5 | As 4. Also clarifies contract assumptions (e.g. currency, hours, invoicing) without derailing the call. Honest about other processes and timelines. Questions show research into the team or product. |

## Red flags (checklist, not scored)

This is a checklist, not a 1–5 score. Report each one detected, with evidence from the transcript. Items drawn from recruiter and career-center guidance [K08][K14][K15]:

- Misunderstands the role (e.g. talks only about frontend for a backend role) [K08].
- Vague skill descriptions that can't be pinned down after follow-up [K08].
- Quantified claims that sound unverifiable or inconsistent with the CV [K08].
- Unexplained gaps or inconsistent dates [K08].
- Speaks negatively about current or former employers [K14][K15].
- Hasn't researched the company ("what do you guys do?") [K14].
- Opens with salary demands before discussing the role [K14].
- No questions when invited to ask [K14].
- Memorized, robotic delivery, or forgets parts of a scripted answer [K14][K08].
- Bluffing on a technical point instead of admitting uncertainty (see `comun/en/recuperacion.md`).
