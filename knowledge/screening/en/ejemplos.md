## About these examples

**ILLUSTRATIVE — invented examples.**
- The candidate, companies, projects and numbers below are fictional.
- They show a generic junior backend engineer. They are not taken from any real interview or from the user's CV.
- They show patterns to score against `rubrica.md`. They are not scripts to memorize; career centers advise against memorizing answers word for word [K04][K05].

Word counts are given so the speaking time can be estimated. ⚠ sin fuente — heurística: at roughly 130–150 words per minute, 150–200 words takes about 60–90 s. See `form_targets.yaml` for the sourced speech-rate references.

## Tell me about yourself — strong example (ILLUSTRATIVE)

> "I'm a backend developer with about two years of experience, mostly in Node.js and TypeScript. Right now I work at a small logistics startup. I build and maintain the REST APIs our mobile app uses: order tracking, payments callbacks, and the Postgres data model behind them.
>
> Before that, I did a one-year internship where I wrote integration tests and fixed production bugs. That's where I learned to read logs and metrics before touching code. Last year I cut the p95 response time of our tracking endpoint roughly in half by adding an index and caching one expensive query.
>
> For the past few months I've been learning Rust on my own. I built a small CLI that parses and validates JSON events, because I want to work closer to systems where performance and correctness matter.
>
> That's why this role caught my attention: it's backend work in Node.js and Rust, on infrastructure that has to be reliable. And I'd like to grow with a remote team."

**Why it's strong (rubric notes):**
- **clarity_structure 5.** Present → past → future [K04]. The close links the story to the role.
- **conciseness 4–5.** About 170 words, inside the 60–90 s target [K05][K06]. No life story [K07].
- **experience_role_alignment 4–5.** Concrete backend work, its own scope, and one result. The new skill (Rust) is presented honestly as self-learning.
- **motivation_company_knowledge 3–4.** It ties to the role's stack. To score a 5, it would add one specific fact about the company's product.

## Tell me about yourself — weak example (ILLUSTRATIVE)

> "Okay, so, um, I was born in a small town and I always liked computers, since I was a kid I played a lot of video games. Then I studied engineering, it was a long degree, like six years, and I did a lot of things, Java, Python, some frontend, some backend, databases, a little bit of everything. Then I worked in a company, it was a consultancy, I did different projects, the projects were for clients, and well, I learned a lot. I'm a hard worker, a team player, I like challenges. And now I'm looking for new opportunities because I want to grow. I think that's it."

**Why it's weak (rubric notes):**
- **clarity_structure 1–2.** It is chronological from childhood with no main point. Career centers say the question is not a request for your life story [K07].
- **experience_role_alignment 1–2.** It is a list of technologies without one thing built or one result. Recruiters probe exactly this kind of vague skill description [K08].
- **motivation_company_knowledge 1.** "New opportunities" could fit any company [K14].
- **Other issues.** Soft-skill claims without evidence. A weak close ("I think that's it"). Fillers at the start ("okay, so, um").

## Compensation — strong example (ILLUSTRATIVE)

Recruiter: "What are your rate expectations for this contract?"

> "Based on what I've researched for remote junior backend contract roles with Node.js and some Rust, I'm looking at a range of [X] to [Y] US dollars per month, as a full-time contractor. I'm flexible within that range depending on the overall package and the hours. Could you share the budgeted range for this role, so I can see how it compares?"

**Why it's strong (rubric notes):**
- **professionalism_logistics 5.** The range is grounded in research [K16]. It states the unit (per month), the currency and the contract assumption (full-time).
- It signals flexibility, and the bottom of the range should still be acceptable to the candidate [K17].
- It invites the recruiter's band, a recognized strategy [K17], in a collaborative tone [K16].
- **conciseness 5.** About 70 words, around 30 s.
- ⚠ sin fuente — heurística: replace [X]–[Y] with figures from your own market research. Do not take numbers from this example.

## Compensation — weak example (ILLUSTRATIVE)

> "Hmm, I don't know, whatever you think is fair. Well, actually, I need at least [Z] because of my expenses, but, like, I could maybe accept less, it depends. What do you usually pay?"

**Why it's weak (rubric notes):**
- **professionalism_logistics 1–2.** There is no researched basis [K16]. The answer anchors on personal expenses instead of market value. The unit and currency are missing.
- It concedes before any negotiation ("I could maybe accept less"). That works against the range strategy, where the floor is still acceptable [K17].
- **communication_english.** The hesitation markers ("hmm", "like", "well, actually") are fine one at a time, but clustered they suggest the candidate didn't prepare.

## How the simulator should use these examples

- Use the strong examples as reference points for scores of 4–5. Use the weak ones for scores of 1–2.
- Compare the candidate's answer **pattern by pattern** (structure, scope, evidence, close), not by wording.
- When giving feedback, quote the candidate's own sentence and suggest a rewrite in their own context. Don't paste these sample answers.
