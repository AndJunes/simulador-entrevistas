## Tell me about yourself

**Why it's asked.** It usually opens the interview. It is a short introduction tailored to the listener, not your life story [K02][K07].

**What a strong answer contains:**
- **Present:** your current role or focus (e.g. junior backend developer, the stack you use now).
- **Past:** 1–2 relevant experiences or projects and the skills you gained [K04].
- **Future:** why this role and company is the logical next step [K04].
- Language from the job posting (Node.js, Rust, APIs, distributed systems) where it is true for you [K04].
- No full biography, no repeat of the CV line by line, and no personal or family details [K05][K07].

**Target length:** 60–90 s [K05][K06]. MIT gives 30–60 s for a generic elevator pitch [K03]; for a screening "tell me about yourself", use the 60–90 s version.

## Why do you want to work here? / Why this company?

**Why it's asked.** Recruiters probe motivation and retention risk. They look for reasons beyond pay and location [K08].

**What a strong answer contains:**
- A specific reason tied to the company: its product, technical domain or mission, showing you researched it [K07].
- A link between that reason and your skills or goals. For example, an interest in backend reliability, in Rust's safety guarantees, or in the domain the company works in.
- Something about the role itself, not only the company.

**Target length:** ~45–75 s. ⚠ sin fuente — heurística.

## What do you know about what we build?

**Why it's asked.** It checks preparation and whether you understand the role [K08]. Not knowing what the company does is a classic mistake [K14].

**What a strong answer contains:**
- In plain words, what the product does and who uses it.
- One concrete detail: a product, an open-source repo, a technical blog post or a recent announcement.
- Where a junior backend engineer would fit, phrased as a hypothesis ("I imagine the backend team works on…; is that right?").
- Honesty about the limits of public information. Do not invent internal details.

**Target length:** ~45–60 s. ⚠ sin fuente — heurística.

## Walk me through a backend project you built

**Why it's asked.** Recruiters want candidates who can clearly describe what they did and how they applied their skills [K08]. The O*NET tasks for software developers give the content to cover [K11]:
- requirements;
- design;
- testing;
- fixing errors;
- performance.

**What a strong answer contains:**
- Context in one or two sentences: what the system did and for whom.
- **Your** role, using "I" for your contribution [K01].
- Stack and key decisions: language, framework, database, API style, deployment.
- One technical challenge and how you handled it.
- A result: shipped, users, latency, error rate, or what you learned. Quantify if you honestly can [K01][K02].
- Recruiter-friendly level: explain the "what" and the "why" before the "how".

**Target length:** 60–120 s, then let the recruiter ask follow-ups. ⚠ sin fuente — heurística. A career-center STAR handout caps example stories at about 2 minutes [K18].

## What's your experience with Node.js / Rust / [technology X]?

**Why it's asked.** It confirms that skills on the CV are real. SHRM advises recruiters to probe vague skill descriptions [K08].

**What a strong answer contains:**
- Honest level and duration ("about a year with Node.js in production; Rust in personal projects for three months").
- One concrete thing built with it.
- For a skill still being learned: what you have done to learn it, and how fast. Active learning is a core skill in the O*NET profile [K11].
- No inflated claims. Recruiters verify specific claims [K08].

**Target length:** 30–60 s per technology. ⚠ sin fuente — heurística.

## Tell me about a bug or incident you solved

**Why it's asked.** Fixing errors and improving performance are core developer tasks [K11]. It is a behavioral question, so answer it with STAR (see `star/en/`).

**What a strong answer contains:**
- **Situation:** what broke, and the impact.
- **Task:** your responsibility.
- **Action:** how you diagnosed it (logs, reproduction, metrics, bisecting) and fixed it.
- **Result:** what changed, plus the prevention step you added (test, alert, postmortem).

**Target length:** 60–90 s (the user's prep target). The MIT time split is ~60 % on the Action [K01].

## Availability, contract type and time zone

**Why it's asked.** Logistics are part of the screen: location and work arrangement [K08]. For a LATAM contract role, this also covers time-zone overlap.

**What a strong answer contains:**
- A concrete start date or notice period.
- Your time zone and the hours of overlap you can commit to.
- Understanding of the contract model (contractor, invoicing, hours). Ask a clarifying question if it is unclear.
- ⚠ sin fuente — heurística: confirm any assumptions instead of guessing (e.g. "Is this full-time hours as a contractor?").

**Target length:** 15–30 s. ⚠ sin fuente — heurística.

## What are your rate / salary expectations?

**Why it's asked.** Recruiters ask about compensation expectations on screens [K08].

**What a strong answer contains:**
- A **researched range**, not a guess. Use market data and your network, and consider total compensation, not only the headline number [K16].
- A range set so that its bottom is still acceptable to you [K17].
- Optionally, ask for the role's budgeted range first, then respond [K17].
- For a contract: state the unit (hourly or monthly) and currency. ⚠ sin fuente — heurística.
- A calm, collaborative tone, leaving room for the other side to respond [K16].

**Target length:** 15–40 s. ⚠ sin fuente — heurística.

## Are you interviewing with other companies?

**Why it's asked.** Recruiters assess timelines and retention risk [K08].

**What a strong answer contains:**
- An honest, brief answer: yes or no, and at what stage, without naming companies unless you choose to.
- Any deadline that matters ("I may have an offer by…").
- A reaffirmation of interest in this role, if it's true.

**Target length:** 10–25 s. ⚠ sin fuente — heurística.

## Do you have any questions for me?

**Why it's asked.** It shows interest and preparation. Prepare 3–5 questions, and avoid anything the website already answers [K13]. Answering "No" is a listed mistake [K14].

**Strong questions for a recruiter screen** [K13][K38]:
- "What does success look like in the first 30–90 days for this role?"
- "Which tools and technologies does the backend team use day to day?"
- "How does the team collaborate across time zones?"
- "What are the next steps in the process, and what's the timeline?"

**Target length:** each question ~10–15 s. Keep 2–3 for a 15-minute call. ⚠ sin fuente — heurística for the count; [K13] suggests preparing 3–5.

## Follow-up you can't answer on the spot

If you get a technical question you can't answer, don't bluff. Don't end on a bare "I don't know" either [K37]:
- Say what you do know.
- Explain how you would find out or reason through the problem.
- Bridge to related experience.
