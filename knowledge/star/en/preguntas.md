## How this bank is organized

The questions are grouped by competency. The competencies come from three places:
- **O*NET's task and skill profile for software developers** [K11]: analyzing requirements; conferring with engineers on interfaces and performance; modifying software to fix errors; testing; reporting status. Skills include critical thinking, active learning and speaking. Work styles include adaptability, attention to detail and dependability.
- **Google re:Work's hiring attributes** [K09]: role-related knowledge, problem solving, and emergent leadership for individual contributors.
- **Amazon's Leadership Principles** [K12], used as one well-known example of how a company names behavioral competencies.

Questions follow OPM's model: a past-behavior prompt that asks who was involved, what you did and what resulted, plus planned probes [K10].

For every question, a strong answer is a specific past event told in STAR form, with "I" actions and a measurable result where possible [K01][K02]. One story can serve several competencies [K02].

## Problem solving and debugging

O*NET core task: modifying software to correct errors or improve performance [K11]. Google: problem solving [K09]. Amazon: Dive Deep [K12].

**Questions**
- Tell me about the hardest bug you've debugged. How did you find the root cause?
- Describe a time a production service failed or degraded. What did you do?
- Tell me about a time you improved the performance of an API or query.

**Strong answer contains**
- Your diagnostic steps: reproduce, logs, metrics, hypotheses tested.
- Why you chose them.
- The fix.
- A measured result (error rate, latency).
- A prevention step.

**Typical probes** [K10]: How did you know it was fixed? What would you do differently?

## Ownership and initiative

Amazon: Ownership, Bias for Action [K12]. Google: emergent leadership for individual contributors [K09]. O*NET work style: dependability [K11].

**Questions**
- Tell me about a time you took responsibility for something outside your assigned tasks.
- Describe a time you noticed a problem nobody had asked you to fix.

**Strong answer contains**
- What you noticed.
- Why it mattered.
- What you did without being asked.
- How you kept others informed.
- The outcome.

## Learning a new technology quickly

O*NET essential skill: active learning [K11]. Amazon: Learn and Be Curious [K12]. This competency is highly relevant for a Node.js developer moving into Rust.

**Questions**
- Tell me about a time you had to learn a new language or framework quickly to deliver something.
- How did you get productive in an unfamiliar codebase?

**Strong answer contains**
- The concrete learning strategy (docs, small prototypes, reading code, asking for review).
- How long it took.
- What you shipped with the new skill.

## Collaboration and disagreement

O*NET task: conferring with engineers and analysts to design systems and clarify interfaces [K11]. Amazon: Earn Trust; Have Backbone, Disagree and Commit [K12]. Google's sample behavioral question probes how teammates responded [K09].

**Questions**
- Tell me about a time you disagreed with a teammate on a technical decision.
- Describe a time you worked with a frontend or mobile team to define an API contract.

**Strong answer contains**
- Your position and the evidence behind it.
- How you listened.
- How the decision was made.
- How you supported it afterwards.
- No blame on others.

## Deadlines and prioritization

O*NET transferable skill: time management. O*NET task: judging whether a design is feasible within time and cost limits [K11]. Amazon: Deliver Results [K12].

**Questions**
- Tell me about a time you had more work than time. How did you prioritize?
- Describe a time you had to cut scope to meet a deadline.

**Strong answer contains**
- The criteria you used.
- Who you consulted.
- What you deferred and why.
- What was delivered on time.

## Quality and testing

O*NET task: developing testing or validation procedures [K11]. O*NET work style: attention to detail [K11]. Amazon: Insist on the Highest Standards [K12].

**Questions**
- Tell me about a time a test or a code review caught a serious problem.
- Describe a time you improved the reliability or test coverage of a service.

**Strong answer contains**
- The specific practice you introduced or followed.
- What it caught or prevented.
- Its measurable effect.

## Receiving feedback

O*NET work style: adaptability [K11]. Google's sample question asks about the impact of your behavior on the team [K09].

**Questions**
- Tell me about a piece of critical feedback you received on your code. What did you do with it?

**Strong answer contains**
- The feedback, stated without defensiveness.
- What you changed.
- Evidence that you kept doing it.

## Communicating with non-technical people

O*NET essential skills: speaking, writing; reporting project status [K11].

**Questions**
- Tell me about a time you had to explain a technical problem or delay to a non-technical stakeholder.

**Strong answer contains**
- How you adapted the message: impact first, little jargon.
- What decision it enabled.
- The outcome.

## Failure and mistakes

Amazon: Earn Trust, which includes being self-critical [K12]. OPM's result probe asks what you would do differently [K10].

**Questions**
- Tell me about a mistake you made that affected others. How did you handle it?
- Describe a project that didn't go as planned.

**Strong answer contains**
- A real mistake, owned in "I" form.
- Immediate mitigation.
- Communication.
- The lasting change you made.

Avoid fake weaknesses or blaming circumstances.
