## "We" instead of "I"

**The mistake:** the whole story is told as "we decided, we built, we fixed". The interviewer can't tell what the candidate personally did. MIT CAPD lists overusing "we" as a mistake, because it hides your individual skills. It recommends "I" statements that show your specific contribution, even in team efforts [K01][K02].

**Detection signal:** the Action section is mostly first-person plural, with few or no "I" verbs.

**Fix:**
- Keep "we" for the team context.
- Switch to "I" for your own steps: "We had a failing deploy. I checked the logs, I found…".

## No result, or a vague result

**The mistake:** the story ends at the action ("…and then I deployed the fix"), or with "it went well". The Result component should explain the outcome and, ideally, a quantifiable one [K01][K02]. OPM's result probes ("What was the outcome?") exist because candidates often stop early [K10].

**Fix:**
- End with a concrete outcome: what changed, by how much, for whom.
- If there was no number, give a verifiable fact (released, adopted, incident closed) and what you learned.

## Too long, especially the Situation

**The mistake:** spending half the answer on background, then rushing the action. MIT's guide gives the Situation about 20 % and the Action about 60 % [K01]. CUNY's handout recommends keeping a STAR story to about 2 minutes and rehearsing aloud to stay under that [K18]. Rambling is a classic interview mistake [K14].

**Detection signal:**
- Total duration over the target in `form_targets.yaml`.
- The first "I" action word appears late in the transcript.

**Fix:**
- Situation and Task in 2–3 sentences.
- Get to "what I did" within about 15–20 s. ⚠ sin fuente — heurística, derived from the 20 % share of a 60–90 s answer.

## Generic or hypothetical instead of a real example

**The mistake:** answering "Tell me about a time…" with "I usually…" or "I would…". Behavioral questions ask for a specific past event, because past behavior is used to predict future behavior [K10]. MIT lists vague answers without specifics as a common mistake [K01]. UT Austin's guide says to prove skills with a concrete occasion, not just claim them [K07].

**Fix:**
- Name a specific project and moment.
- If you truly have no example, say so honestly. Then offer the closest real experience, or how you would approach it (see `comun/en/recuperacion.md`).

## Saying what you think they want to hear

**The mistake:** polishing the story into something that didn't happen. That includes inventing results, inflating your role, or hiding real difficulties. MIT advises being truthful about your actual behaviors and emotions, and notes that exaggeration can seem disingenuous and may not match your résumé or references [K01]. Recruiters verify quantifiable claims and dates [K08].

**Detection signal:** low `cv_consistency`. The scope or claims don't match the CV.

**Fix:** tell the real story with honest scope. A modest, true result scores better than an implausible one.

## Memorized, scripted stories

**The mistake:** reciting a fixed script. It sounds unnatural and breaks when the question is phrased differently. MIT warns that memorized stories limit adaptability and can sound unnatural [K01]. UNI lists memorized answers where parts get forgotten [K14].

**Fix:**
- Memorize the story's skeleton: one line each for S, T, A and R, plus the key numbers.
- Practice telling it in different lengths and for different questions [K02].

## Yes/no answers to closed questions

**The mistake:** "Have you ever worked under a tight deadline?" → "Yes." Then silence. MIT recommends a "yes, and…" approach: answer, then give an example [K01]. CUNY's handout makes the same point [K18].

**Fix:** "Yes. For example, last March…", then STAR.

## Story doesn't match the question or the job

**The mistake:** telling a favorite story that shows a different competency, or one unrelated to backend work. MIT advises connecting examples to the skills in the job description [K01]. UNI lists failing to connect your skills to the employer's needs [K14].

**Fix:**
- Before answering, name the competency to yourself: "This is about conflict", or "this is about learning".
- Pick the story from your bank that best shows it.
- Close with one sentence that ties it back.

## Blaming others

**The mistake:** in conflict or failure stories, making a colleague, manager or client the villain. Speaking negatively about past employers or colleagues is a listed interview mistake [K14].

**Fix:** describe the disagreement factually. Focus on your actions and what you learned.
